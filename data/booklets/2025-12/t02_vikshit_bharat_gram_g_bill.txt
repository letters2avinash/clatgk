Key Features  
  
Employment Guarantee: 125 Days of Guaranteed Unskilled Manual Work per Rural Household per Financial Year (Earlier: 100  
days under MGNREGA)  
  
e State Governments empowered to Suspend Scheme for up to 60 Days during Sowing and Harvesting Seasons to ensure  
Availability of Agricultural Labour.  
  
Funding Responsibility Shift: From an Open-Ended, Predominantly Central — funded Wage Model (MGNREGA) to a Centrally  
  
Sponsored Scheme with Shared Financial Responsibility.  
  
¢ States/UTs— 60% Centre : 40% State  
  
e Northeastern & Himalayan States — 90% Centre : 10% State  
  
Durable Asset Creation (Priority Themes): Works must align with 4 National Priorities: Water Security, Core Rural Infrastructure,  
Livelihood-related Infrastructure, Special Works for Extreme Weather Events.  
  
Unemployment Allowance: Maintains Provision for an Unemployment Allowance if Work is Not provided Within 15 Days ofa  
Household's Application.  
  
Planning Architecture: Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans (VGPPs) prepared by Gram Panchayats.  
¢ VGPPs integrated with PM Gati Shakti for Infrastructure Convergence.  
  
o Plans aggregated through Viksit Bharat National Rural Infrastructure Stack (VB — NRIS) for Coordinated National

Grading of Panchayats based on Development Indicators.  
o Higher Allocations for Underperforming Panchayats to Reduce Regional Disparities.  
  
Institutional Oversight  
  
Central & State Gramin Rozgar Guarantee Councils: Review, Monitor & Advise on Scheme Implementation.  
  
National & State Steering Committees: Recommendations on Financial Allocation, Inter — Ministerial Convergence and  
Strategic Oversight.  
  
Benefits of VB— G RAM G Bill  
  
e Increased Employment Guarantee: Statutory Work Guarantee raised from 100 to 125 Days Per Household  
Annually, potentially Boosting Rural Incomes and Livelihood Security.  
  
e Faster Wage Payments: Envisages Weekly Wage Payments, Improving Liquidity for Workers compared to 15 —day  
Cycle under MGNREGA.  
  
e Support for Agriculture: Pause of Public Works for up to 60 Days during Sowing / Harvest ensures Farmers have  
access to Adequate Labour.  
  
e Focus on Water Security: Dedicated Water — related Works improve Irrigation Infrastructure and Drinking Water  
Availability in Rural Areas.  
  
e Technological Integration: Mandates a Digital Ecosystem, including Biometric Authentication and AI Analytics, to  
Reduce Leakages, Improve Transparency and Track Work Effectively.

VB—G RAM G Bill at a glance  
  
A look at the key provisions of the Viksit Bharat—Guarantee for  
Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) or VB—G RAM G Bill, 2025,  
being brought by the Centre to replace the Mahatma Gandhi National  
  
Rural Employment Guarantee Act,  
  
What's the bill?  
  
VB- G RAM G Bill, 2025 proposes to  
establish a modern statutory framework  
aligned with Viksit Bharat 2047,  
guaranteeing 125 days of employment per  
rural household whose adult members  
volunteer to do unskilled manual work.  
  
What makes it better  
than MGNREGA?  
  
© The new bill represents a major  
upgrade over MGNREGA, fixing  
structural weaknesses while enhancing  
employment, transparency, planning,  
and accountability.  
  
© It proposes guaranteed 125 days of  
wage employment a year, up from 100  
days under MGNREGA, giving rural  
households higher income security.  
  
© While MGNREGA works were scattered  
across many categories, the new bill  
focuses on four major types of works:  
ensuring durable assets that directly  
support water security, core rural  
infrastructure, livelihood-related  
infrastructure creation and climate  
adaptation.

Why shift to normative funding?  
  
© Ademand-based model leads to unpredictable  
allocations and mismatched budgeting.  
Normative funding uses objective parameters,  
ensuring predictable, rational planning while  
still guaranteeing that every eligible worker  
receives employment or unemployment  
allowance.  
  
What transparency measures are  
  
built into the new bill?

verticals for rural development; enhanced  
monitoring role for panchayats; GPS/mo-  
  
bile-based monitoring; real-time MIS dashboards;  
  
weekly disclosures; and stronger social audits.
