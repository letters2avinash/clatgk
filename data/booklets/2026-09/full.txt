leverage over regional maritime traffic and intensifying tensions involving Yemen, Saudi Arabia, Iran and other regional  
powers. The development therefore has implications not only for Yemen but also for global trade, energy security and Red  
Sea maritime security.

India Rejects Hague  
Court Ruling on  
Indus Waters Treaty  
  
Why in News?  
  
India has rejected a ruling by Hague — based Court of Arbitration (CoA) concerning Indus Waters Treaty, 1960. India  
  
termed tribunal improperly constituted and maintained that CoA lacks jurisdiction over India's Sovereign Decision to  
place treaty in Abeyance following April 2025 Pahalgam terrorist attack.  
  
Development has renewed debate over Dispute — Resolution Architecture of IWT, particularly Parallel Proceedings  
initiated by India and Pakistan.  
  
66 India has rejected the Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA) ruling on the 1960 Indus Waters Treaty (IWT),  
maintaining that the treaty remains in force from India's perspective and that the tribunal lacks jurisdiction because it was  
  
Cliffs Notes /  
  
illegally constituted.  
  
The PCA in The Hague unanimously held that the Indus Waters Treaty remains legally binding and that unilateral  
suspension or placing it “in abeyance” is not permissible under international law. It also imposed interim restrictions on  
India's Ratle Hydroelectric Project (RHEP), prohibiting concreting of specified portions of the dam wall and power intake  
structure beyond prescribed levels until 90 days after a final decision by a Neutral Expert, expected around July 2027. The  
tribunal further stated that the treaty regulates water rights and does not incorporate security or terrorism concerns,  
meaning geopolitical developments do not legally justify suspending treaty obligations.  
  
India's Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) rejected the ruling, stating that the Court of Arbitration was constituted by the  
World Bank contrary to the treaty's provisions. India has neither recognized the tribunal's legitimacy nor participated in its  
proceedings. New Delhi reiterated that its decision to place the treaty in abeyance following the April 2025 Pahalgam  
terror attack remains valid. India also asserts that the tribunal has no jurisdiction over its sovereign decisions or  
infrastructure projects on the western rivers.

Person—to—Merchant (P2M) UPI Transactions above 22,000.  
New Framework will come into effect from 15 Oct 2026.  
AnMDR of 0.4% will apply to eligible High— Value Merchant UPI Transactions.  
  
zero-MDR policy, introduced in 2020, exempted merchants from paying any fee for UPI and RuPay transactions to  
encourage digital adoption, particularly among small businesses and informal sectors.  
  
66 UPI has grown into a large-scale digital payment infrastructure handling billions of transactions every  
month. With the expansion of the network, payment infrastructure requires continued investment in technology,

cybersecurity, fraud prevention, server capacity and customer support.  
The new MDR framework provides a revenue mechanism within the payment ecosystem while keeping everyday low-  
value transactions free. NPCI and the government have also emphasised the need to protect small merchants and ordinary  
  
users from additional costs.

Major Types of Satellite Orbits  
  
e An Orbit is Curved Path followed by a Satellite around Earth under influence of gravity.  
  
¢ Choice of Orbit determines Geographical Coverage, Image Resolution, Revisit Time, Communication Delay, Fuel  
Requirement and Mission Application.  
  
1\. Low Earth Orbit (LEO)  
e = Altitude: Approx 160 — 2000 km above Earth.  
e — Orbital Period: Around 90 — 120 minutes.  
  
e Key Features: Low Communication Latency, High — resolution Earth Observation Capability, Suitable for Large  
Satellite Constellations and Crewed Platforms such as International Space Station.  
  
¢ — Important Subtypes:  
o Sun -— Synchronous Orbit: Provides relatively Consistent Solar Illumination for Observations.  
o Very Low Earth Orbit: Enables Very High — Resolution Observations but experiences Greater Atmospheric Drag.  
2\. Medium Earth Orbit (MEO)  
e = Altitude: Approx 2000 km to 35786 km.  
e Key Features: Provides a balance between Coverage Area and Signal Latency.  
¢ Major Applications: Primarily used by Global Navigation Satellite Systems.  
o Examples include GPS Constellation and European Union's Galileo System.

e Located at approx 35,786 km above Earth.  
  
e A Satellite in this orbit has an Orbital Period equal to Earth's  
Rotational Period (23 Hrs, 56 Mins, 4 Secs).

appear Stationary over One Location. -
