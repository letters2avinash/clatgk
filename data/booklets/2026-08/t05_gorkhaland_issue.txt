Major Bases of Demand  
  
e Ethno — Linguistic Identity: Supporters argue that Population of Darjeeling Hills possesses a Distinct Linguistic, Cultural  
and Social Identity compared with Bengali— majority Plains.  
  
oc. Region has a predominantly Nepali—speaking Population.  
  
o Concerns over Identity Insecurity and Racial Stereotyping have contributed to demands for Stronger Political  
Recognition & Representation.  
  
co Proponents emphasise that Movement seeks Recognition of Community as an Integral Part of India, rather than  
Separation from the Country.  
  
e Economic and Developmental Concerns: Region has historically raised concerns regarding Inadequate Infrastructure,  
Limited Employment Opportunities, Developmental Neglect, Insufficient Political and Fiscal Autonomy.  
o Supporters also point to Region's Contribution through sectors such as Darjeeling Tea, Forestry and Tourism.  
  
Institutional Arrangements  
i e Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council (1988): Established following Gorkhaland Agitation of 1980s to provide Greater  
i Autonomy to Hill Region.  
o However, its effectiveness was constrained by Limited Legislative Authority, Inadequate Financial Autonomy,  
Dependence on State Government.  
¢ = Gorkhaland Territorial Administration (2011): Gorkhaland Territorial Administration was established as a  
  
More Empowered Autonomous Arrangement.  
  
o It received Greater Administrative, Executive and Financial Responsibilities compared with DGHC.  
  
Challenges in Resolving Gorkhaland Issue  
  
e Competing Sub — National Aspirations: Gorkha demands for Greater Political Autonomy intersect with Strong Opposition in  
parts of West Bengal to further Territorial Division.  
  
o Concerns include State Territorial Integrity, Economic Interests, Administrative Cohesion, Distribution of Natural and  
Economic Resources.  
e Strategic Importance of Region: Darjeeling Region occupies Siliguri Corridor (Chicken's Neck), a Sensitive Geographical  
Position close to Nepal, Bhutan and Bangladesh.  
o Corridor provides Principal Land link between Mainland India and Northeast.  
o Consequently, Prolonged Political Instability in region can have Wider Strategic and National — Security Implications.  
e Fragmented Political Representation: Gorkha Political Landscape comprises multiple organisations and political groups,  
including Gorkha Janmukti Morcha and Gorkha National Liberation Front.  
o Differences among Political Stakeholders make it difficult to establish a common position on what should constitute a  
“Permanent Political Solution”.  
e Economic Sustainability: Any New Political or Administrative Arrangement would need a Viable Economic Foundation.  
o Regional Economy remains significantly dependent on Tea, Tourism and related activities, which face Structural and  
  
Seasonal Challenges.
