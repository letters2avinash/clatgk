Why in News?  
  
Supreme Court of India ruled that Parental Income alone cannot be used as Sole Criterion for determining Creamy Layer  
Status of Individuals belonging to Other Backward Classes (OBCs).  
  
Creamy Layer in OBC Reservation  
  
Creamy Layer Principle identifies and excludes Socially and Economically Advanced Members of OBC  
Communities from Reservation Benefits.  
  
A Family is classified as Creamy Layer if its Annual Income exceeds \*8 Lakh for 3 Consecutive Years.  
  
Established by Supreme Court in Indra Sawhney vs Union of India (1992 Mandal Commission Case).  
  
Purpose: Ensures that Reservatiag Benefits reach Most Socially and Educationally Disadvantaged Sections  
  
Key Observations of Supreme Court  
  
e Post — based Criterion: For Parents employed in Public Sector Undertakings or Private Sector, Category of Post (Group A, B,  
C or D) should be Primary Factor for determining Creamy Layer Status.  
  
e Current System: Children of Government Employees are evaluated based on Parental Rank or Post.  
  
o Children of PSU and Private — Sector Employees are assessed primarily on Parental Income.  
  
e Court noted that this Unequal Method of Assessment creates Inconsistency in determining Creamy Layer Status.  
  
o Treating Children of PSU or Private — Sector Employees differently from Government Employees violates Principle of  
Equality guaranteed under Article 184, 15 and 16.  
  
Evaluation of Ruling  
Social Status Indicator: Rank or Position in Employment better reflects Social Status and Influence than Income alone.  
Social Mobility through Service: Long Years of Service may increase Salary Levels without necessarily improving  
Social Standing, especially for Lower-—level Employees.  
  
Uniform Evaluation Standard: A Post — based Criterion applied across All Sectors can create a Fair and Consistent  
Method of Identifying Creamy Layer.  
  
Concerns and Criticisms  
  
Absence of Standardised Hierarchy: Unlike Government Services, Private Sector lacks a Uniform Rank  
Hierarchy, making Post — based Classification Difficult.  
  
Income as a Key Determinant: Critics argue that Income significantly determines Access to Quality  
Education and Opportunities, which should remain a major factor.  
  
Reservation Access Issues: High — Income Families in Private Sector could potentially Retain Reservation  
Benefits, thereby limiting opportunities for Economically Weaker OBC Groups.

Historical Background of Reservation: Pre — Independence  
  
Concept of Reservation based on Caste was 1" proposed by “William Hunter” and “Jyotirao Phule” in 1882.  
British Raj introduced elements of Reservation in Government of India Act 1909 by giving “Separate Electorates”’.  
“MacDonald/Communal Award” provided for Separate Electorates for Depressed Classes in 1932.  
  
o However, “Poona Pact” abandoned Separate Electorates for Depressed Classes.  
  
Government of India Act of 1935 extended Communal Representation by giving Separate Electorates to Depressed Classes,  
Women and Labour.  
  
Historical Background of Reservation: Post — Independence  
  
1951: 1" Constitutional Amendment added Article 15(4) in Constitution to legalize Caste — based Reservation in India.  
o Champakam Dorairajan vs State of Madras (1951) Case: Court ruled that Caste — based Reservations are in Violation of  
  
Article 15 of the Constitution.  
  
Kalelkar Commission 1953 (1" Backward Class Commission) was 1" to identify Backward Classes other than Scheduled  
Castes and Scheduled Tribes at National Level.  
  
In MR Balaji v State of Mysore (1963), Court ruled that Public Administration Efficiency is Critical & ordered Government  
  
to keep Reservation at 50%.  
  
In 1979, Morarji Desai Government appointed “2™ Backward Classes Commission” under chairmanship of BP Mandal, in  
terms of “Article 340” of Constitution to investigate Conditions of Socially and Educationally Backward Classes and suggest  
measures for their advancement.  
  
o Submitted its report in 1980 and identified 3743 Castes as Socially and Educationally Backward Classes.  
o Recommended for “Reservation of27% Government Jobs” for Other Backward Classes.  
  
o Mandal Commission recommendation of 27% Reservation for OBCs was implemented in 1990 (VP Singh Government)  
  
In 1991, Narasimha Rao Government introduced two changes:  
  
1\. Preference to the Poorer Sections among the OBCs in the 27% Quota, i.e., Adoption of the Economic Criteria in granting  
Reservation.  
  
2\. Reservation of Another 10% of Jobs for Poorer (Economically Backward) Sections of Higher Castes who are Not  
covered by Any Existing Schemes of Reservation.  
  
Indra Sawhney Case Judgement (Mandal Case) 1992: Court has rejected Additional Reservation of 10% for Poorer Sections  
  
of Higher Castes, it upheld Constitutional Validity of 27% Reservation for OBCs with certain conditions, viz,  
  
o Reservation shall Not exceed 50%.  
  
o Reservation in Promotions shall Not be allowed.  
  
o “Concept of a Creamy Layer” was introduced, and it was directed that such a Creamy Layer be excluded while  
identifying Backward Classes.  
  
o “Carry Forward Rule” in case of Unfilled (Backlog) Vacancies is Valid, but Criteria of 50% rule should Not be violated in  
Any Case.  
  
o A Permanent Statutory Body should be established to examine complaints of Over — Inclusion and Under — Inclusion in  
  
the List of OBCs.  
  
2006: Nagaraj Case Judgement: Supreme Court ruled that “State was Not bound to provide Reservation in Promotions to  
SCs/STs”.
