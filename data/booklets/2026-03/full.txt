Important Note for

How to use the magazine  
  
The magazine consists of topics that hold relevance and importance in relation to CLAT.  
  
Each topic has following parts:  
  
1,  
  
News: This section ensures that the readers know what part of the topic has made it  
  
to the headlines ofa newspaper  
  
Cliffs Notes : This section helps the reader to know the context of the topic.  
Explanation that will help the reader understand about the topic is the motive of this  
section of the magazine. Before cramming the facts and details about the topic it is  
  
essential that you understand the topic. A little detail has never hurt anybody.  
  
Skill up : This section contains those topics which are related to the main topic  
discussed throughout the page. A little information on the periphery helps you  
answer those questions that are not part of the daily news but have crawled into the  
exam paper to fetch you another mark. Topics that are on the side of the main news  
have often helped in gaining that last mark which eventually puts you into the final  
  
selection list ofthe CLAT.  
  
Practice Questions: This section has a set of questions to help you gauge your  
understanding of the topic. You should try and solve the questions once you have  
thoroughly done the topic. On some days, start with the questions to test yourself for  
that one situation in the CLAT when you know nothing about the topic, but you will  
have to guess the answers. On a side note, some questions might come across to you  
  
as a surprise but then when has the CLAT main exam not thrown us off our seats\!  
  
Happy Learning\!

SC RULING ON CREAMY

Why in News?  
  
Supreme Court of India ruled that Parental Income alone cannot be used as Sole Criterion for determining Creamy Layer  
Status of Individuals belonging to Other Backward Classes (OBCs).  
  
Creamy Layer in OBC Reservation  
  
Creamy Layer Principle identifies and excludes Socially and Economically Advanced Members of OBC  
Communities from Reservation Benefits.  
  
A Family is classified as Creamy Layer if its Annual Income exceeds \*8 Lakh for 3 Consecutive Years.  
  
Established by Supreme Court in Indra Sawhney vs Union of India (1992 Mandal Commission Case).  
  
Purpose: Ensures that Reservatiag Benefits reach Most Socially and Educationally Disadvantaged Sections  
  
Key Observations of Supreme Court  
  
e Post — based Criterion: For Parents employed in Public Sector Undertakings or Private Sector, Category of Post (Group A, B,  
C or D) should be Primary Factor for determining Creamy Layer Status.  
  
e Current System: Children of Government Employees are evaluated based on Parental Rank or Post.  
  
o Children of PSU and Private — Sector Employees are assessed primarily on Parental Income.  
  
e Court noted that this Unequal Method of Assessment creates Inconsistency in determining Creamy Layer Status.  
  
o Treating Children of PSU or Private — Sector Employees differently from Government Employees violates Principle of  
Equality guaranteed under Article 184, 15 and 16.  
  
Evaluation of Ruling  
Social Status Indicator: Rank or Position in Employment better reflects Social Status and Influence than Income alone.  
Social Mobility through Service: Long Years of Service may increase Salary Levels without necessarily improving  
Social Standing, especially for Lower-—level Employees.  
  
Uniform Evaluation Standard: A Post — based Criterion applied across All Sectors can create a Fair and Consistent  
Method of Identifying Creamy Layer.  
  
Concerns and Criticisms  
  
Absence of Standardised Hierarchy: Unlike Government Services, Private Sector lacks a Uniform Rank  
Hierarchy, making Post — based Classification Difficult.  
  
Income as a Key Determinant: Critics argue that Income significantly determines Access to Quality  
Education and Opportunities, which should remain a major factor.  
  
Reservation Access Issues: High — Income Families in Private Sector could potentially Retain Reservation  
Benefits, thereby limiting opportunities for Economically Weaker OBC Groups.

Euthanasia  
  
Refers to Intentional Ending of a Person's Life to relieve suffering caused by Incurable Illness or  
Unbearable Pain.  
  
o Generally conducted under Medical Supervision to ensure Dignity at end of life.  
  
Active Euthanasia: Deliberate Intervention to cause Death such as administering a Lethal Injection.  
  
o Legal Status in India: Illegal and Punishable.  
  
Passive Euthanasia: Withdrawing or Withholding Life — Sustaining Treatment, including Ventilators,  
Artificial Nutrition or Hydration, Life — support Medication.  
o Legal Status in India: Permitted.  
  
Legal Framework for Passive Euthanasia in India)  
  
e Legal Recognition: Recognised by Supreme Court of India in Common Cause vs Union of India (2018), which allowed  
Withdrawal or Withholding of Life — Sustaining Treatment under regulated conditions.  
  
e Constitutional Basis: Grounded in Article 21 of Constitution of India, which guarantees Right to Life with Dignity.  
  
e = Living Will: Individuals may draft a Living Will specifying that Life — support Treatment should Not be continued if they  
become Terminally III or enter a Persistent Vegetative State.  
  
e Medical Board Approval: Withdrawal of Treatment requires evaluation by Primary Medical Board (Doctors from Treating  
Hospital) and by Secondary Medical Board (Independent Medical Specialists for Additional Review).  
  
e Judicial Oversight: Decision must be communicated to a Judicial Magistrate to ensure Legal Transparency and Oversight.  
  
e Applicability: Passive Euthanasia is allowed Only when Patient is Terminally Il or In a Persistent Vegetative State with No  
Reasonable Chance of Recovery.  
  
Evolution of Supreme Court Guidelines  
e =Aruna Shanbaug vs Union of India (2011: Supreme Court I" recognised Passive Euthanasia as Legally  
Permissible in India.  
o However, Euthanasia was Not granted in Shanbaug's Case.  
@ = Common Cause v. Union of India (2018): Courtruled that Right to Die with Dignity forms Part of Article 21.
