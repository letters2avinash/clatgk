How to use the magazine  
  
The magazine consists of topics that hold relevance and importance in relation to CLAT.  
  
Each topic has following parts:  
  
1,  
  
News: This section ensures that the readers know what part of the topic has made it  
  
to the headlines ofa newspaper  
  
Cliffs Notes : This section helps the reader to know the context of the topic.  
Explanation that will help the reader understand about the topic is the motive of this  
section of the magazine. Before cramming the facts and details about the topic it is  
  
essential that you understand the topic. A little detail has never hurt anybody.  
  
Skill up : This section contains those topics which are related to the main topic  
discussed throughout the page. A little information on the periphery helps you  
answer those questions that are not part of the daily news but have crawled into the  
exam paper to fetch you another mark. Topics that are on the side of the main news  
have often helped in gaining that last mark which eventually puts you into the final  
  
selection list of the CLAT.  
  
Practice Questions: This section has a set of questions to help you gauge your  
understanding of the topic. You should try and solve the questions once you have  
thoroughly done the topic. On some days, start with the questions to test yourself for  
that one situation in the CLAT when you know nothing about the topic, but you will  
have to guess the answers. On a side note, some questions might come across to you  
  
as a surprise but then when has the CLAT main exam not thrown us off our seats\!

Katchatheevu Island Dispute  
  
Katchatheevu is an Uninhabited 285 — acre Island located in Palk Strait  
between India and Sri Lanka.  
  
Ownership of Katchatheevu, once part of Madras Presidency, remained a  
Point of Dispute between India and Sri Lanka until 1974.  
  
o Ceylon's (Sri Lanka) Claim: Initially ruled by Jaffna Kingdom of Sri  
Lanka in Early Medieval Period.  
  
o Sri Lanka claimed Sovereignty over Katchatheevu on ground that  
Portuguese who had occupied Island during 1505 — 1658 CE had  
exercised jurisdiction over the islet.  
  
o. Madras (India's) Claim: Control later shifted to Ramnad Zamindari  
  
under Nayak Dynasty of Madurai in 17" Century.  
  
Indo — Sri Lankan Maritime Agreement 1974: India Government, headed  
by Indira Gandhi, agreed to cede Katchatheevu Island to Sri Lanka.  
  
o Aimwasto Settle Discord and Strengthen ties with Sri Lanka.  
  
o Agreement allowed Indian Fishermen Continued Access to  
  
Katchatheevu.  
  
Additional Agreement of 1976: Prevented Both Countries from Fishing in  
Each Other's Exclusive Economic Zones, with Katchatheevu positioned at  
Boundary of these zones.  
  
Post — 2009 Sri Lanka Civil War, tensions rise over Indian Fishermen in Sri  
Lankan Waters, leading to Arrests, Torture Allegations and renewed  
Katchatheevu demands.

OVERSEAS CITIZEN OF INDIA (OCI)  
  
A foreign national, who was eligible to become citizen of India  
on 26.01.1950 or was a citizen of India on or at anytime after  
26.01.1950 or belonged to a territory that became part of India  
after 15.08.1947 is eligible for registration as Overseas Citizen  
of India (OCI). Minor children of such person are also eligible  
for OCI. However, if the applicant had ever been a citizen of  
Pakistan or Bangladesh, he/she will not be eligible for OCI.  
  
BENEFITS  
  
OCIs are entitled to a multipurpose, multiple entry, lifelong visa  
  
allowing them to visit India at any time, for any length of time  
  
and for any purpose  
  
« Exempted from police reporting for any length of stay in the  
country  
  
« Have also been granted all rights in the economic, financial  
and education fields in parity with NRIs except, the right to  
acquisition of agricultural or plantation properties

Cliffs Notes /  
  
A Uniform Civil Code is seen as a Progressive Goal for a Secular Nation, but its implementation  
  
Uttarakhand's UCC aims to standardize laws on Marriage, Divorce and Succession, making it the Ist State,  
after Pre — Independence Goa, to adopt sucha code.  
  
However, Provision mandating registration of live-in relationships, with a three-month prison penalty for non-  
registration, is deemed intrusive and unconstitutional. While code has positive aspects, such as granting  
legitimacy to children from live-in relationships and ensuring maintenance in cases of desertion, its  
interference with personal rights is concerning.  
  
Historically, Adoption ofa UCC has been controversial, with figures like B.R. Ambedkar advocating for it to be  
voluntary. Current Law Commission has revived the idea, gathering public views.
