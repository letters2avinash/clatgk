Grants — in — Aid  
  
'e Constitution provides for Grants — in — Aid to State from Central Resources.  
  
i e Two types of Grants — in — Aid: Statutory Grants and Discretionary Grants  
o Statutory Grants (Art 275): “Parliament” to make grants to “States” which are in “Need of Financial  
Assistance” and “Not to Every State”.  
\= These sums are “Charged on Consolidated Fund of India” every year.  
\= These are given to “States” based on “Recommendation of Finance Commission”.  
o Discretionary Grants +(Art 282): Both Centre and States to make Any Grants for Any Public Purpose,  
even if it is not within their legislative competence.  
\= Centre makes grants to States under this clause.  
  
\= Centre is under No Obligation to give these grants and the matter lies within its Discretion.  
  
Extra Shots  
be Cess: A "Tax on Tax" levied by Central Government for specific purposes (e.g., education, health).  
o Funds must be used for Specified Purpose and are deposited in Consolidated Fund of India.  
o Mandatory for All Taxpayers (Individuals, Companies, Firms).  
i o Not Shared with State Governments.  
e Surcharge: An Additional Tax on top of Base Tax liability for High — Income Earners or Entities, aimed at  
i increasing Tax Burden on the rich.  
o Generally applies when Income exceeds €50 lakh for Individuals/HUFs and Higher Thresholds for  
Companies.  
o Not Shared with State Governments and is at Exclusive Disposal of Union Government.  
SASCI: Launched in 2020 — 21, provides Long — Term, 50 — Year Interest — Free Loans to State  
Governments for Capital Projects.
