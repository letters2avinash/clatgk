International Emergency Economic Powers Act 1977  
  
e Enacted by President Jimmy Carter in 1977 to replace Trading with Enemy Act (1917).  
e Primarily a Sanctions Tool, Nota General Tariff Law.  
  
e Empowers U.S. President to act when there is an Unusual and Extraordinary Threat originating Outside United States that  
threatens National Security, Foreign Policy, Economic Stability  
  
e President must formally declare a National Emergency before invoking IEEPA.  
e Key Powers Granted  
o Regulate or Prohibit International Financial Transactions.  
o Freeze Foreign Assets in US.  
o Block Trade and Commercial Dealings with Specific Countries, Entities or Individuals.  
o Restrict Imports or Exports in Certain Emergency Contexts.  
  
e Historically Use: Since its enactment, 77 National Emergencies declared under IEEPA; 46 Remain Ongoing as of | Sept  
2025\. E.g.: Economic Sanctions Against Countries (e.g., Iran, North Korea, Russia)

| Prior to Trump, no U.S. President had employed International Emergency Economic Powers Act as a mechanism to  
impose Trade Tariffs. :

National Emergency, citing: i  
o = - Harmful Foreign Trade and Economic Practices affecting U.S. Economy.  
  
o —\_ Lack of Reciprocity in Trade Relations with Other Nations.  
  
Objective: To establish a Legal Foundation under IEEPA to implement Broad Tariffs on Imports, leveraging
